Big statement

Hello investors — the global toy, board-game and puzzle category sells billions of flat, single-purpose products every year, yet consumers are demanding more replayability, customization and creator-led design than legacy manufacturers can deliver. The physical-product opportunity now sits where additive manufacturing, a mature creator economy and a distinctive hardware form factor can meet in one product.

Note: market-size statistics require independently sourced research; the hook above is a defensible qualitative statement. See the Source Register.

2

Problem

  • Flat boards and single-use puzzles get played once and shelved.
  • Consumers want customization but legacy toy/puzzle production can't economically do one-off designs.
  • Independent artists and brands have no clean path from a design idea to a shelf-ready physical product.
  • Collectors want premium, limited editions but mass-market manufacturers won't run short batches.
  • Industrial contract-manufacturing shops can't efficiently handle low-volume prosumer work.
3

Solution

  • Flat, play-once boards → one spherical hardware platform that runs seven distinct game and puzzle experiences.
  • No cost-effective customization → customer-uploaded and paintable editions produced on an additive print farm.
  • No path for creators → creator marketplace with 50/50 net-profit royalty terms.
  • No short-run premium production → numbered limited editions, including future metal pieces.
  • Industrial short-runs underserved → same polymer and metal capacity sold as contract manufacturing.
4

Why now

  • Prosumer additive hardware (Bambu Lab class) has brought per-unit polymer printing costs into range for consumer products.
  • Kickstarter remains the most efficient launch channel for distinctive consumer hardware.
  • The creator economy has normalized royalty-based licensing of independent designs.
  • Metal additive manufacturing has matured enough that short-run specialty parts are commercially addressable.
