# 03M · Market, Competitor & Commercial Validation

**Document scope:** market framing and competitive landscape for Sphere of Kings and the three underserved demand classes the company proposes to address. **No TAM/SAM/SOM figures, growth rates, market shares, or industry statistics appear here unless they can be attributed to a named, dated, publicly verifiable source.** Where such figures are needed for investor conversations, the Company will commission or cite independent research separately.

## 1 · The three market areas (qualitative framing)

### 1.1 · Consumer board-game, puzzle, and collectible products
- **Target customer:** families, gamers, collectors, puzzle enthusiasts, hobbyists, gift buyers, licensed-merchandise buyers.
- **Customer problem:** flat, single-purpose boards and puzzles have low replayability; mass-market manufacturers do not economically produce short-run premium, customized, or creator-designed editions.
- **Proposed solution:** one spherical hardware platform running seven distinct game and puzzle experiences, with customization (custom-upload, DIY paint, creator editions) and a long tail of themed limited editions.
- **Purchase behaviour:** primarily seasonal and gift-driven; collectors buy outside gift windows; hobbyists and families repeat-purchase through expansion packs and themed editions.
- **Competitive alternatives:** established board-game publishers (Hasbro, Mattel, Ravensburger, Buffalo Games), specialty collectibles (Funko, WizKids), and the broader puzzle category. SOK does not claim parity on channel scale; it claims differentiation on form, modularity, and the manufacturing stack that supports short-run customization.
- **Pricing considerations:** flagship 7-in-1 at ~$179 retail; board-only ~$129; expansion packs $12–$79. Margin is the dominant lever; BOM validation required.
- **Sales channel:** Kickstarter (launch 2026-11-19) → DTC on Sphereofkings.com → specialty retail → national retail → international, each gated on validated demand.
- **Market-size evidence required:** independent board-game / puzzle / collectibles market report with a named, dated source. **PENDING.**
- **Commercial validation required:** Kickstarter prelaunch sign-ups, first-48-hour backer rate, first-SKU sell-through, repeat-purchase measurement on expansion packs. **PENDING.**
- **Risks:** channel margin compression at retail scale; defect rate; IP challenge; toy-safety compliance for children's editions.

### 1.2 · Creator-economy physical-product licensing
- **Target customer:** independent artists and designers with original designs; brands, sports organizations, and entertainment IP holders wanting themed physical editions.
- **Customer problem:** designers have no clean path from a design to a shelf-ready, manufactured physical product with transparent royalty accounting.
- **Proposed solution:** Creator marketplace — SOK accepts qualifying designs, manufactures and distributes them, and pays a **50/50 split of contractually defined net profit** to the creator. Rights, exclusivity, territory, and term are per-contract.
- **Purchase behaviour:** two-sided — creators onboard and submit designs; end buyers purchase the resulting limited editions.
- **Competitive alternatives:** print-on-demand services (Redbubble, Etsy), merchandising platforms (Spring, Teespring), licensing agencies.
- **Market-size evidence required:** independent creator-economy / licensed-merchandise market data with named, dated source. **PENDING.**
- **Commercial validation required:** at least one signed creator agreement + one shipped creator-edition SKU before claiming traction.
- **Risks:** chicken-and-egg (needs brand gravity to attract creators); rights-handling disputes; marketplace-platform execution.

### 1.3 · Short-run polymer and metal contract manufacturing
- **Target customer:** consumer-goods product companies needing low-volume runs or customization; industrial prototype shops; educational and R&D organizations; replacement-parts programs; (later) qualified industrial accounts including tooling / fixtures, specialty automotive, aerospace tier-2/3 (demanding qualification), medical devices (regulated).
- **Customer problem:** short-run polymer and metal industrial work is underserved — large bureaus prioritize high-volume contracts; in-house capacity is expensive and idle outside a company's own product cycle.
- **Proposed solution:** sell the same polymer-print-farm and metal-additive capacity that produces SOK editions as contract manufacturing to outside customers, coordinated through the Division D software platform (quoting / MES / royalties).
- **Competitive alternatives:** Protolabs, Xometry, Shapeways, Formlabs service bureaus, regional CNC/IM shops; HP and EOS service partners for metal.
- **Market-size evidence required:** contract manufacturing / additive-services market data with named, dated source. **PENDING.**
- **Commercial validation required:** first signed industrial pilot customer (gate for Stage 2 → Stage 3). **PENDING.**
- **Risks:** multi-year qualification lead times; equipment underutilization; skilled-operator shortage; material-cost volatility; metal-powder hazards.
