# Sphere of Kings — Executive Summary

**Creative Design Networks** · Proposed $100M expansion strategy · 2026–2031
**For investor discussion only.** Figures are illustrative targets, not forecasts. The $100M is a capital-raising target, not confirmed funding.

---

## The opportunity

Sphere of Kings (SOK) is a spherical, modular gaming system with seven product experiences: **Royal Checkers, Imperial Kings, Pre-designed Puzzle, Custom Upload Puzzle, DIY Paint Puzzle, Paintable Royal Checkers, and Paintable Imperial Kings.** It launches on Kickstarter **November 19, 2026** under the parent entity Creative Design Networks.

The investment proposition is not simply a game. It is a staged plan to use SOK as the consumer anchor for a vertically integrated advanced-manufacturing enterprise organized around four divisions:

| Division | Role | Status today |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Sphere of Kings Gaming** | Physical gaming products, creator collectibles, and digital companion play | Product line designed; Kickstarter planned 2026-11-19 |
| **B. Polymer Manufacturing & Mass Production** | Industrial polymer 3D-printing farm, injection molding, assembly, contract production | Proposed; no farm-scale capacity today |
| **C. Advanced Metal Manufacturing** | Metal additive, CNC, tooling, specialized industrial parts | Proposed; no equipment ordered |
| **D. Technology, Licensing & Digital Platforms** | Design-intake → quoting → MES → royalties; creator marketplace; digital gaming | Platform scope drafted; software not yet built |

## The capital plan

A **proposed** $100 million staged over five tranches, each gated by measurable milestones rather than released on a calendar.

| Stage | Capital | Primary objective |
|---|---:|---|
| 1 · Validation | $2M | Prove product economics and production reliability |
| 2 · Commercial launch | $8M | Establish initial warehouse manufacturing |
| 3 · Industrial expansion | $20M | High-volume equipment; qualified metal pilots |
| 4 · Regional scaling | $30M | Expand contracted manufacturing capacity |
| 5 · Global expansion | $40M | Scale proven operations; selective international markets |
| **Total** | **$100M** | **Five-year program** |

## Illustrative financial ambition (not a forecast)

- **Year 5 revenue ambition:** $250M (Gaming $100M · Polymer/mass-prod $80M · Metal $50M · Licensing/digital $20M).
- **Year 5 EBITDA ambition:** $50M (20% margin).
- **Illustrative pre-money valuation:** $300M, giving a $100M investor a 25% stake at $400M post-money. Not an independent valuation; subject to negotiation.
- **Illustrative investor outcomes** at Year-5 exit, no dilution: $500M exit → 25% ROI; $1B → 150% ROI; $2B → 400% ROI. These are mathematical sensitivities, **not promises**.

## What must happen before the figures become credible

1. Bottom-up financial model replacing top-down divisional ambitions.
2. Equipment supplier quotations for Stage-1 and Stage-2 deployment.
3. Smaller initial pilot facility — not a 150,000 sq. ft. flagship — selected on documented cost comparison.
4. Signed customer evidence (gaming retailers and at least one industrial manufacturing LOI).
5. Formal IP and corporate structure review by qualified counsel.
6. Documentary evidence of any Rabbit Capital / Shark Ready engagement.

## Why these divisions belong together

The thesis is that linking consumer-demand generation (Gaming) with flexible production capacity (Polymer, Metal, CNC) and connective software (Licensing/Digital) creates two revenue engines from one capital footprint: SOK-branded products flow through the same equipment that also serves industrial contract-manufacturing customers. Investors should evaluate each division on its own economics before concluding that the combination is more valuable than specialization — the Blueprint is explicit about that test.

## Key risks

- **Metal additive manufacturing** is high fixed cost; must be entered selectively with validated demand.
- **Gaming unit volume** at a $100M target requires ~750K units/year at a $130 blended ASP — a demanding commercial build.
- **Facility scale** — a 150K sq. ft. first warehouse is almost certainly premature.
- **Capital release discipline** — staged financing must be contractually gated; later stages should not auto-release.
- **IP and corporate ownership** — any ambiguity blocks institutional financing.
