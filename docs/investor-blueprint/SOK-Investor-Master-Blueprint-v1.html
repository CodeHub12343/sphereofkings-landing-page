3 · Vision, mission, and long-term opportunity

Vision

To build a vertically integrated creative-manufacturing enterprise that treats the spherical game board as the first in a long-tail of physical and digital products, served by a flexible production network capable of manufacturing both SOK-branded items and outside industrial contracts on the same equipment.

Mission

Combine distinctive, collectible consumer products with disciplined, qualified industrial production. Use software to connect design intake, pricing, scheduling, quality, royalties, and fulfillment across both consumer and industrial customers.

The long-term opportunity

The strategic logic is that most consumer-product businesses either (a) outsource manufacturing and lose margin and control, or (b) build captive manufacturing that is idle outside of their own product's demand cycle. The Company's thesis is that a flexible print-and-mold network can be utilized by SOK's own products and by outside contract customers, giving the manufacturing division diversified demand that is not exposed to the risk of a single gaming SKU's success or failure.

Thesis under test Investors should evaluate whether running Gaming + Manufacturing + Licensing + Technology together creates more value than running four specialized businesses. This document is structured so that each division can be evaluated on its own economics first, and the integration benefit second. The Company does not claim integration benefits are automatic.

What success looks like

  1. Year 1 — Validation. Sphere of Kings is manufactured, delivered, and sold at a positive contribution margin; Kickstarter cohort is fulfilled; investor-ready financial records exist.
  2. Year 2 — Commercial launch. A right-sized warehouse operation produces at planned yield; initial industrial contract customers have been signed on a pilot basis.
  3. Year 3 — Industrial expansion. High-volume manufacturing is operating; the metal pilot has produced qualified parts for a first set of industrial customers.
  4. Year 4 — Regional scaling. Contract manufacturing capacity has been expanded; licensing revenue is growing from authorized creator programs.
  5. Year 5 — Global expansion. Proven operations scale into selective international markets; the enterprise generates sustainable operating cash flow.

5 · Strategic investment thesis

Thesis in one paragraph

A branded consumer product (Sphere of Kings) provides the initial demand, the visible brand, and the first production commitments. The same manufacturing capacity — polymer printing, injection molding, CNC, metal additive — is sold to outside industrial customers to fill utilization gaps and diversify revenue. A software layer coordinates both. Licensing and digital products are the long-tail extension of the brand. If any single division underperforms, the others can continue; if every division performs, the whole is larger than the sum of its parts.

Five reasons the thesis is worth testing

  1. Differentiated consumer anchor. The spherical form factor and seven-in-one design are distinctive in a category (board games, puzzles, collectibles) that rewards novelty.
  2. Flexible production economics. Additive manufacturing supports customization at low minimum volume; mass production handles the standard SKUs; metal additive opens premium and industrial markets.
  3. Two-sided demand. The manufacturing division is not reliant on a single SKU's success; it can be sold as contract manufacturing independently.
  4. Licensing optionality. A creator marketplace and brand-licensing program create high-margin recurring revenue once the production stack is built.
  5. Software compounding. A design-to-production platform has operating-leverage and acquisition-target properties if it reaches enough users.

Five reasons an investor should be cautious

  1. Divisional complexity. Running four distinct businesses from one holding company demands operating discipline that most seed-stage companies do not possess.
  2. Industrial-manufacturing lead times. Building a qualified industrial customer pipeline typically takes years and does not respond to consumer marketing tactics.
  3. Metal additive capital intensity. Equipment, materials, safety, qualification, and skilled operators are expensive; the division must be entered selectively.
  4. Facility and fixed-cost risk. A premature 150,000 sq. ft. warehouse would consume capital before revenue justifies it.
  5. Founder concentration. At this stage, delivery depends heavily on the founder; investor protection must include governance and a credible leadership-build plan.

The decisions investors are asked to make

  • Participate at Stage 1 ($2M) under agreed milestones and governance.
  • Reserve pro-rata rights on later stages, subject to performance.
  • Approve an initial governance structure that grants investor-side board rights appropriate to the stage.
  • Approve a documented capital-release schedule rather than a lump-sum commitment.

6 · Four-division ecosystem overview

How the divisions reinforce each other

  • Gaming → Manufacturing: Sphere of Kings orders provide the initial production commitment that justifies equipment purchases.
  • Manufacturing → Gaming: In-house capacity gives SOK control over cost, quality, customization, and lead time; outside contract revenue absorbs overhead that would otherwise burden gaming unit cost.

  • Licensing → Both: Authorized creator designs run through the same production stack and generate royalty revenue with minimal marginal fixed cost.
  • Technology → All: A shared design-to-production platform is the only scalable way to coordinate consumer and industrial demand against the same equipment.

Make-or-buy worked example: flagship 7-in-1 game board

Illustrative Suppose the 3D-printed board component costs $30 in direct manufacturing (polymer, machine time, finishing, labor). Suppose the injection-molded equivalent costs $12 per unit but requires $180,000 of tooling. The per-unit saving is $18. Break-even units = $180,000 ÷ $18 = 10,000 units (ignoring financing, storage, design changes, scrap differences, and tool maintenance). Under these hypothetical assumptions, injection molding is less expensive after ~10,000 accepted units. This illustrates why the business retains flexible 3D printing for customization while moving proven, high-volume products to more economical mass-production methods.

9 · Division C — Advanced Metal Manufacturing

What this division does

Metal additive manufacturing (laser powder-bed fusion class), CNC machining, tooling, industrial prototyping, and qualified specialized metal components. In the integrated ecosystem it also produces premium metal editions of Sphere of Kings game pieces and collectibles.

What exists today

Status No metal additive equipment has been ordered. No qualified industrial customer agreements exist. No hazard assessment has been performed on any candidate facility for metal-powder operations. This division is entirely prospective as of the date of this Blueprint.

Why metal is in the plan at all

  1. Premium brand extension. Metal pieces, tokens, and collector editions command premium pricing and reinforce the SOK brand.
  2. Industrial margin. Qualified metal additive services sell at substantially higher margins than polymer printing.
  3. Facility and overhead absorption. Once the polymer side is operating, an adjacent metal cell can share facilities, finance, HR, and software overhead.

Why metal must be entered selectively

  • Equipment cost per LPBF system is typically in the seven figures installed.
  • Metal powders have serious hazard profiles — combustible dust, inert atmospheres, dedicated rooms, dedicated HVAC, explosion protection, trained personnel.
  • Customer qualification (aerospace, medical, automotive) is expensive and multi-year.
  • Underutilized LPBF capacity is a cash-flow drag the business cannot absorb in its first two years.

14 · Manufacturing process selection and make-or-buy

A major component of the investment strategy is choosing the most economical manufacturing method for each SKU rather than assuming every part should be 3D printed. The following decision framework is applied to every SKU.

Decision tree (summary)

  1. Can we subcontract this at acceptable landed cost and quality? If yes, subcontract until internal demand justifies equipment.
  2. Is annual accepted volume < the break-even against tooling? If yes, retain additive / small-batch methods.
  3. Does the part have precision or finish requirements beyond additive capability? Add CNC or finishing steps.
  4. Is annual accepted volume > break-even AND SKU design stable for >24 months? Invest in tooling for injection molding.
  5. Is the part high-value and amenable to additive? Evaluate metal additive against subcontracted CNC.

Capacity model requirements

Each equipment class is to be modeled on the following inputs before capital release: machine hours per shift, cycle time per part, scrap rate, operator hours per completed unit, material cost per unit, overhead allocation, target utilization at 1/2/3-shift operation, output per machine per day, and expected OEE. These inputs are the basis of the capacity model required in the Investor Readiness Checklist.

Quality management system

Documented procedures covering: incoming material inspection, first-article approval, machine calibration, process validation, in-process inspection, final inspection, lot/machine/material traceability, corrective action, customer complaints, and continuous improvement. ISO 9001 certification is a target for general manufacturing; aerospace, medical, or automotive customers will require additional specialized qualifications, which are not claimed to be in place today.

Supplier and material strategy

  • At least two qualified suppliers for every critical material class.
  • Documented acceptance criteria and incoming-inspection plans.
  • Material traceability linked to work orders and finished-goods lots.
  • Hazardous-material storage and handling per applicable regulations.
  • Price-volatility exposure review quarterly; hedging considered where material becomes a dominant cost line.

TermMeaning
